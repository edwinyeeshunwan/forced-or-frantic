# Data limitations that the supplied local files cannot resolve

1. **No record identifiers.** The normalised liquidation files keep only `ts, coin, side, direction, px, sz, notional, is_liquidation`. Trade IDs, order IDs, transaction hashes and user addresses were dropped when the files were created. The fills cannot be reconciled one-to-one with the Reservoir or Hyperliquid archives.
2. **Rows removed by the legacy normaliser.** `pull_hl_reservoir.py` applied full-row `drop_duplicates()` after discarding identifiers. The retained pandas index shows at least 31,253 BTC rows (6.1%) and 24,500 ETH rows (8.1%) were removed. Each removed row was identical to a retained row on every kept field, including the millisecond timestamp. They may have been genuine repeated archive rows or distinct fills that happened to match (for example equal-sized matches at one price). Only the raw archive can tell. Sensitivity S9 reinstates them under an adjacent-row assumption. Rows removed after the last retained index would be invisible.
3. **Feed coverage.** Every one of the 153 calendar days from 1 August to 31 December 2025 has rows, but there is no within-day coverage metadata. 88–90% of five-minute bins contain no liquidation, and zero-liquidation runs reach 25 hours. Quiet markets and missing data cannot be told apart. Sensitivity S8 treats zero runs of 12 hours or more as missing. Hyperliquid's documentation warns that archived data may be missing or delayed.
4. **Partition semantics.** Within the files the side mapping is internally consistent: every sell closes a long, every buy closes a short, and no fill appears on both sides. What cannot be verified locally is whether the Reservoir liquidation partition includes every liquidation route, such as backstop or vault transfers and partial liquidations, and what the few `Liquidated Cross/Isolated` direction labels mean. Those labels appear on four BTC dates and six ETH dates, mostly 10 October 2025.
5. **Price series.** The five-minute bars were derived from one-minute asset-context price snapshots. The ingestion code prefers `mid_px`, then `mark_px`, then `oracle_px`, but which field was used, and whether that changed over time, is not recorded in the stored files. High and low are extremes of at most five snapshots, not traded extremes, and volume is missing throughout. The bars are not an executable trade-price series.
6. **Snapshot timing.** OI and price snapshots are assumed to reflect state at their timestamp. 65 OI rows sit off the one-minute grid, and 116 grid minutes are missing (79 in July 2025, before any event window, and 37 scattered over 21 later dates). Archive publication latency is not recorded, so "available at" is a research-time convention, not a real-time guarantee.
7. **Funding and ADL.** Funding values are asset-context rates, not realised account payments. The ADL files contain events on a handful of dates only. Neither can independently validate a forced-flow mechanism.
8. **Sample.** One venue, two assets, one five-month period. A full 30-day baseline leaves onsets from 1 September to 31 December 2025. Nothing here is out-of-sample.
